Sunlight Hasa Sweet Spot

A solar cell turns each photon above its band gap into one electron and loses every photon below it. Push the gap down and the voltage collapses; push it up and the light walks straight through. Shockley and Queisser worked out the best possible balance. Under real sunlight it sits at a gap of 1.34 eV, where at most 33.68 % of the light can become electricity.

Screening means asking a cheap question many times and an expensive question a few times. A machine-learning model guesses the gap of every molecule in QM9; quantum chemistry then checks the few that land closest to the sweet spot.

Why a laptop? Because that is what a student has, and because it makes the cost of every calculation honest. Everything here ran on one machine: Intel Core i5-10500H (6 cores), 16 GB RAM, 6 OpenMP threads. Its processor is rated at 45 W, which is how the last experiment puts a price on knowing.

Treat it as a toy

This is the Ridge model, the weakest of the three: test MAE 0.603 eV, against 0.226 eV for gradient boosting.

It was trained on QM9 molecules with at most 9 heavy atoms. On real dyes (HOPV15) its mean error is 12.01 eV, and it predicts gaps as low as −32.16 eV.

Treat it as a toy.

The ray ends in the near-infrared, at the wavelength of the target gap: λ = hc / E = 925 nm for 1.34 eV (derived).
