Descriptors& Filters

RDKit • Lipinski • Dye-likeness

Which cheap, countable features of a molecule track its band gap, and which molecules look like dyes at all?

What we did

Eleven descriptors per molecule, all computed with RDKit: size, polarity, rings, flexibility, complexity, and one of our own, the size of the largest conjugated system. Then two filters: Lipinski's rule of five, and a five-rule dye-likeness filter written for this project.

What we found

The conjugated-system size tracks the gap best (Pearson −0.721), with the fraction of sp³ carbons close behind in the other direction (0.712). Our filter keeps 13,087 QM9 molecules and 303 of the 350 real dyes.

Our dye-likeness filter

our own construction by analogy with Lipinski; not a literature standard

  1. largest conjugated system >= 6 heavy atoms (at least a benzene ring's worth)

    QM9 21,654 · HOPV15 348

  2. at least half the heavy atoms are in that conjugated system; single bonds between conjugated rings are allowed (Part 3: flattening one costs < kT)

    QM9 31,619 · HOPV15 345

  3. contains at least one electron-donor group AND one electron-acceptor group

    QM9 27,045 · HOPV15 305

  4. no 3- or 4-membered rings, no O-O, N-N, non-nitro N-O single bonds, no azide

    QM9 50,339 · HOPV15 350

  5. no formal charges except inside a nitro group (no zwitterions)

    QM9 130,388 · HOPV15 350

Molecules passing each rule on its own. All five together: 13,087 in QM9, 303 in HOPV15.

Honest negative · the wrong yardstick

Lipinski's rule is for pills, not dyes.

Every QM9 molecule passes it (100.0 %), because they are all tiny. Only 26.9 % of real solar dyes do. Drug-likeness says nothing about light harvesting, which is why we wrote a dye filter.
