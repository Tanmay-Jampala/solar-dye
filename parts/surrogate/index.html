TheSurrogate

Ridge • Random forest • HistGB • QM9

Can a model learn the gap from cheap descriptors, and use it to find a dye in QM9?

Best test error (gradient boosting)
0.226 eV
Closest prediction to the target
3.10 eV
Same model on real dyes
3.61 eV

What we did

Three regressors learned the QM9 gap from the eleven descriptors: Ridge, a random forest and gradient boosting. The split was random 80/10/10, seed 42 (justification in src/model.py): 104,665 molecules to train, 13,083 to choose settings, 13,083 touched once at the end.

The best model then ranked every dye-filter passer by its distance from the 1.34 eV target, and B3LYP checked the top 3.

What we found

Gradient boosting is best, at 0.226 eV mean error on the test set. Its picks hold up under B3LYP: mean error 0.208 eV.

But the closest prediction is 3.10 eV, still 1.76 eV short of the target.
