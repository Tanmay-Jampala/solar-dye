Brus • CdSe • PbS

Instead of a molecule, could a tiny crystal be tuned to the target gap just by its size?

What we did

A semiconductor crystal a few nanometres across squeezes its electrons, and the squeeze widens the gap. The Brus equation adds that confinement energy to the bulk gap and subtracts the electron–hole attraction. We solved it for CdSe and PbS from 1.00 nm to 6.00 nm and compared it with experimental sizing curves.

What we found

PbS can be tuned to the target: Brus says a radius of 3.00 nm. CdSe cannot: its bulk gap is already above the target, and shrinking it only raises the gap.

Honest negative · two of them

CdSe cannot reach the target.

no radius reaches 1.34 eV: the bulk gap (1.74 eV) is already above it and confinement only raises the gap.

For PbS, Brus is off by a factor of 2.00.

Brus puts the target at a radius of 3.00 nm; the measured sizing curve puts it at 1.50 nm, a ratio of 2.00×. The simple effective-mass picture over-confines small, strongly confined PbS dots.
