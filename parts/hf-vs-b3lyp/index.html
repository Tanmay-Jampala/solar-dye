What we did

Nine molecules, from benzene to a three-ring thiophene chain, two push–pull dyes and two dye cores. Each was built from its SMILES, pre-relaxed with a force field, then optimised with Hartree–Fock and with B3LYP in the 6-31G* basis, all on this laptop (Intel Core i5-10500H (6 cores), 16 GB RAM, 6 OpenMP threads).

What we found

Hartree–Fock gaps are far too large: for benzene it is 6.273 eV above B3LYP. B3LYP lands close to QM9's own value for the molecules QM9 contains. Both methods agree on the trend: the longer the conjugated chain, the smaller the gap.

Honest negative · my prediction was wrong

I expected B3LYP to underestimate the optical gap. It overestimates it.

Predicted: B3LYP underestimates the optical gap. Observed: for these short oligomers the B3LYP HOMO-LUMO gap is ABOVE the experimental optical gap, and the excess shrinks as the chain grows. The HOMO-LUMO gap is not the optical gap: it ignores the electron-hole attraction (exciton binding) and B3LYP's self-interaction error, which pull in opposite directions; their balance depends on size.
